The Verdict  /  Yes, AI can help here
You are not short of work. You are short of a way to see the money leaving.

You told us the problem is the pipeline. Your own answers describe a business that wins plenty of work and loses margin after it wins it:

  • The same job prices two ways. Two estimators, two spreadsheets, about eight per cent apart.
  • No job's real margin is visible until the year-end accounts, long after anything can be done about it.
  • Cash you have already earned sits in retentions that nobody is chasing.

Fixing what you keep is faster and cheaper than chasing more of what you win. Everything below sits on an AI Foundation: the business written down in a form AI can use, so the answers are yours rather than generic.

Reading the map High pain, high repetition and low risk moves first. That puts pricing at the top, with job costing straight after it.
Priority 1The same job prices two ways
  • Each estimator builds a quote from their own spreadsheet. Price the same steel package through both and it lands about eight per cent apart.
  • Asked which number would have been right, nobody could say.
Root cause. Pricing lives in two people's heads and a folder of old spreadsheets. There is no single rate card, so the price depends on who picks up the enquiry.
What it costs. On £8.5 million of turnover, a couple of points of drift is roughly £170,000 a year, given away one quote at a time. And if either estimator leaves, the judgement leaves with them.
Our recommendation
Do it yourself, this week. Put both estimators in a room and write one rate card and one standard build-up. It's free, needs no AI, and removes most of the spread on its own.
Then an estimating assistant. It reads the drawings, produces a first-pass take-off and prices it against that rate card, so the estimator checks a draft instead of starting from a blank sheet.
Priority 2You can't see a job's margin until it's over
  • Jobs run over on labour hours and steel wastage.
  • The real margin only appears when the year-end accounts land.
Root cause. No monthly job-costing view. The numbers exist, spread across the accounts and the job records, but nobody pulls them together in time to act.
What it costs. You find out which jobs made money a year too late to change anything, and the thin ones hide inside a healthy-looking annual figure.
Our recommendation
Do it yourself, now. Take the three biggest live jobs and check actual cost against the quote this month, by hand. Rough, but it shows you the leak.
Then an AI Finance Director. Margin by job and by month, where you'll finish the year, and an early warning when a job starts running thin, on the same date every month.
Priority 3Cash is sitting in retentions nobody is chasing
Applications for payment and retention release sit on a spreadsheet only the bookkeeper fully understands. Retentions get chased late, sometimes a year late. That's money already earned.
Our recommendation
Do it yourself. List every retention with its release date and put the dates in the diary. A list and a date, not a project.
Priority 4Most of the work comes from three contractors
It feels like a pipeline problem, and that's what you named. It is also a concentration risk: losing one of the three would hurt more than a slow quarter.
Our recommendation
Not first. Once the margin leaks are closed, make sure Reynolds is named when a contractor asks an AI assistant who fabricates steel in your region. Ask one yourself today to see where you stand. It takes a minute.

Next steps

The first step is a conversation. Whether this reads accurately, what we have got wrong, and which finding matters most from where you sit.

Two things are worth doing whatever you decide, because they cost nothing and don't need us: write the rate card, and put every retention date in the diary.

If you want to take it further, the AI Foundation is where it starts, then the estimating assistant and the monthly job-costing view, in that order.
